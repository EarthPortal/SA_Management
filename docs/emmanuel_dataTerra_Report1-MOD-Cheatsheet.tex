\section{Introduction}

The following is a short report presenting a quick reference table, that I suggest
to accompany the ongoing \textit{Metadata guideline} document, which is currently under review.
Its purpose is to give a submitter a single page that answers three questions at a glance: 
\textit{what must be filled in, what the field is called, and which FAIR sub-principles it supports}.

% ========
\section{Development}

The table covers all 99 attribute entries of the draft, in the document's own order and under its
own twelve section headings. I have corrected a few property names for spelling as I went, and I
made one small choice worth mentioning: \textit{Categories} and \textit{Subjects} currently declare
the same property, so I kept \prop{omv:hasDomain} for \textit{Categories}, which is the portal's own
controlled list, and used \prop{dcterms:subject} for \textit{Subjects}, as the MOD~3.0 column of the
crosswalk~\cite{crosswalk2026} indicates. Before reading the table, a word on the columns.

\begin{description}[leftmargin=2.5cm,style=nextline,font=\normalfont\bfseries]
  \item[Usage] The level in the guideline. \textbf{M}: Mandatory, \textbf{R}: Recommended,
    \textbf{O}: Optional.
  \item[FsF] The level in the FAIRsFAIR minimum metadata profile for semantic artefacts, as
    recorded in our \textit{Metadata attributes} crosswalk~\cite{crosswalk2026}. A dash means the
    crosswalk records no level for that attribute.
  \item[FAIR] The FAIR sub-principles the attribute supports, as annotated in the guideline.
    A dash means the guideline gives none. Values in \added{green} are sub-principles I would
    suggest adding, because the O'FAIRe tool~\cite{amdouni2022faire} reads the attribute when it scores that
    sub-principle, according to its own property configuration~\cite{ofaireconfig}.
  \item[Property] The primary property, as a prefixed name.
\end{description}

\section{Conclusion}

The O'FAIRe tool~\cite{amdouni2022faire} follows MOD~1.4, while our guideline follows MOD~3.0. As a
result, some fields are required by O'FAIRe when it computes the FAIRness score, while our guideline
does not explicitly mention them. For instance, in the following table, I have listed a few cases
among others.

\textbf{Credits} & \textbf{What our guideline has for the same question} \\

  \propb{mod:authorProperty}, \propb{mod:obsoleteProperty} & R1Q1 & 5 &
  nothing: the question rests entirely on these five \\
\propb{mod:ontologyInUse} & R1.3Q1 & 5 & \textit{Endorsed by} (5.7), the other half of the
  question \\
\propb{owl:deprecated} & A2Q4 & 2 & \textit{Status} (1.7), the other half of the question \\
\propb{owl:versionInfo} & R1.2Q4 & 2 & \textit{Prior version} (10.2), which few artefacts fill \\
\propb{mod:hierarchyProperty} & R1Q2 & 1 & \textit{Root of obsolete branch} (11.1); the third
  property of the question, \propb{mod:maxDepth}, is computed by the portal \\

In the light of the previous illustration, I think we definitely need to discuss with Clement's team
and make sure we all agree on how to align the current metric with the guideline.
